# Scene Opportunity Discovery

## Objective

Discover product opportunities from a real-world usage scene before selecting a specific product category or solution.

The purpose is not to brainstorm random products. The purpose is to identify evidence-backed unmet jobs, recurring friction, workarounds, and opportunity spaces within a defined scene.

## Core Principle

Follow:

Scene → People → Activities → Jobs → User Voice → Friction → Workaround → Existing Solutions → Unmet Need → Opportunity Space → Product Concepts → Validation

Do not start with a product or feature unless the user explicitly wants to validate an existing concept.

## Scene Boundary

Define:

- Geography / market
- Physical location
- Time / daypart
- User groups
- Trigger events
- Activities before, during, and after the core scene
- Devices / objects present
- Environmental constraints
- Purchase context where relevant

Avoid making the scene so broad that it becomes meaningless.

## Scene Mapping

Map:

1. Who is present?
2. What are they trying to accomplish?
3. What sequence of actions occurs?
4. Which products or substitutes are used?
5. Where does friction occur?
6. What interruptions, risks, or failures occur?
7. What do users repeatedly check, adjust, carry, remember, clean, charge, organize, or avoid?
8. What workarounds exist?
9. What jobs remain poorly served?

## Opportunity Signals

Give extra attention to:

- Repeated inconvenience
- High-severity failures
- Repeated checking or confirmation behavior
- Manual multi-step routines
- Products being modified by users
- Tape, adapters, spacers, extra cables, improvised storage, or other workarounds
- Multiple products required for one simple job
- Products designed for another scene being repurposed
- High return / complaint themes
- Compatibility friction
- Safety concerns
- Noise, light, heat, size, cable, charging, battery, cleaning, setup, or storage friction
- Users paying a premium for partial solutions
- Important jobs with poor low-cost solutions

## Avoid False Opportunities

Do not treat every inconvenience as a product opportunity.

Reject or downgrade opportunities when:

- Frequency is low
- Severity is low
- Existing solutions already satisfy users
- The problem is easy to ignore
- Users show no behavioral or spending signal
- The solution adds more complexity than value
- The addressable audience is too narrow for the intended business
- Technical, cost, compliance, or manufacturing constraints are prohibitive

## Handoff to VOC

Use `references/voice-of-customer.md` to validate suspected problems with real user voices.

Do not claim a scene observation is a market-wide problem until evidence supports it.

## Required Output for /scene-opportunity

1. Scene Definition
2. User Groups
3. Scene Journey / Activity Map
4. Top Jobs-to-be-Done
5. Existing Products and Substitute Behaviors
6. Friction and Failure Points
7. Workarounds
8. Evidence-backed VOC Themes
9. Unmet Needs
10. Opportunity Spaces
11. Candidate Product Concepts
12. Opportunity Ranking
13. Contradictory Evidence
14. Unknowns
15. Top 3 Opportunities for `/validate`

## Product Concept Rule

Generate product concepts only after identifying the underlying opportunity space.

One opportunity may support multiple product concepts. Do not prematurely select the first obvious solution.

## Example

Scene: US household bedroom nightstand

Do not begin with:

> Wireless charger

Begin with:

- Sleep preparation
- Overnight device charging
- Lighting
- Time checking
- Alarm interaction
- Cable management
- Device placement
- Small-item storage
- Nighttime navigation
- Partner disturbance

Then investigate which jobs contain meaningful unmet needs.
