# Jobs-to-be-Done for Hardware Opportunity Discovery

## Objective

Describe what users are trying to accomplish independently of existing products or features.

## Core Format

When [situation], I want to [motivation / action], so I can [desired outcome].

## Job Types

### Functional Job
What must the user accomplish?

### Emotional Job
How does the user want to feel?

### Social Job
How does the user want to be perceived?

For commodity consumer electronics, prioritize functional jobs unless evidence shows emotional or social jobs materially affect purchase decisions.

## Job Hierarchy

Separate:

- Core job
- Supporting jobs
- Before-job activities
- After-job activities
- Failure-recovery jobs

## Job Mapping

For each job capture:

- Trigger
- Situation
- Desired outcome
- Current solution
- Friction
- Failure consequence
- Frequency
- Importance
- Satisfaction with current solution
- Workaround
- Evidence strength

## Important Rule

Do not encode a solution as a job.

Bad:

> I want a magnetic wireless charger.

Better:

> When I put my phone down before sleeping, I want it to begin charging correctly without careful positioning, so I can go to sleep without checking it repeatedly.

## Opportunity Signal

A strong opportunity often has:

High Importance + Low Satisfaction + Repeated Occurrence + Weak Existing Solution

Use this as a conceptual framework, not fake mathematical precision.

## Outcome Statements

When useful, describe desired outcomes without prescribing implementation.

Examples:

- Minimize the time required to confirm charging has started.
- Minimize the likelihood of overnight charging interruption.
- Minimize unwanted light while sleeping.
- Minimize the number of objects and cables required on a nightstand.
