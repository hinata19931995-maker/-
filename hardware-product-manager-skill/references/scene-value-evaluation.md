# Scene Value Evaluation

## Objective

Determine whether a scene is important enough to shape product strategy and requirements.

Do not assume every observed scene deserves a product or feature.

## Evaluation Dimensions

Evaluate each candidate scene using qualitative evidence first:

- Usage frequency
- Job importance
- Problem severity / failure consequence
- Dissatisfaction with current solution
- Competitive gap
- Solvability
- Willingness-to-pay or behavioral-cost signal
- Market size / reach
- Product fit
- Economic feasibility
- Evidence strength

Use High / Medium / Low / Unknown when evidence is incomplete.

## Conceptual Model

Scene Opportunity = Frequency × Job Importance × Problem Severity × Dissatisfaction × Competitive Gap × Solvability × Economics

Use this only as a reasoning model. Do not invent precision.

## Scene Quality Questions

A strong product-defining scene should answer yes to several of these:

- Does the need reliably become active in this scene?
- Does the user perform a repeatable action sequence?
- Is failure costly, annoying, risky, or emotionally meaningful?
- Is the current solution visibly weak or cumbersome?
- Do users spend time, money, or effort on workarounds?
- Are current competitors weak specifically in this scene?
- Can the product improve the scene without creating more complexity?
- Is the scene common enough for the business model?

## Downgrade Conditions

Downgrade a scene if:

- it is rare
- the job is unimportant
- the problem is easy to ignore
- the behavior is fading
- competitors already solve it well
- the solution requires disproportionate cost or complexity
- the scene is visually attractive but commercially weak

## Required Output

| Scene | User | Timing | Action | Frequency | Job Importance | Problem Severity | Current Satisfaction | Competitive Gap | Economics | Evidence Strength | Role |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Role should be one of:

- Core
- Supporting
- Lead / Beachhead
- Extreme
- Reject

## Handoff

Only Core and strong Lead scenes should materially shape positioning and Must-Win requirements.

Supporting scenes should be considered without overcomplicating the product.

Extreme scenes should primarily inform reliability, safety, and engineering validation.
